Pre-Departure

Everything you need before you fly

🎒
What to Pack ▾
  • Clothing: Light layers for Hanoi & Ha Long. Warm fleece for Sapa — evenings drop to 12°C in October.
  • Footwear: Comfortable walking shoes, water sandals for the cruise, warm socks for trekking.
  • Documents: Passport (6+ months validity), printed e-visa, insurance card, booking confirmation.
  • Health kit: Sunscreen SPF50+, DEET insect repellent, antihistamines, motion sickness tablets.
  • Tech: Universal adapter (Type A/C), power bank, camera with extra memory cards.
  • Money: USD cash easily exchanged at banks. Budget ~$30–50/day personal spending.
  • Temples: Pack a scarf to cover shoulders and knees at religious sites.
🌤
Weather & Climate ▾
  • Hanoi & Ninh Binh: 23–30°C. Occasional light afternoon showers. Mostly clear mornings.
  • Ha Long Bay: 24–28°C. Excellent visibility. October is widely considered the best month to cruise.
  • Sapa: 10–20°C. Crisp mountain air with some morning mist. Bring warm layers.
🌾 Perfect timing: October is rice harvest season in Sapa — the valley turns a spectacular golden colour. You are going at the absolute best time of year.
🛂
Visa Information ▾

Australian passport holders require a Vietnam E-Visa.

  • Apply at: evisa.xuatnhapcanh.gov.vn (official government portal only)
  • Processing time: 3–5 business days. Apply at least 2 weeks before departure.
  • Duration & cost: 90 days, single or multiple entry — ~$25 USD.
  • At immigration: Print your e-visa. Carry your original passport. Have hotel address ready.
⚠️ Avoid third-party visa websites. Only use the official government portal above.
🙏
Cultural Tips ▾
  • Greetings: A nod and warm smile is perfect. Two-handed handshake shows respect to elders.
  • Temples: Remove shoes, cover shoulders and knees, never point feet toward the altar.
  • Tipping: 100,000–200,000 VND/day for your guide; 50,000 VND for drivers.
  • Photography: Always ask before photographing people — especially in Sapa villages.
  • Bargaining: Normal in markets. Be friendly, start at 60–70% of asking price.
  • Table manners: Wait for the eldest to eat first. Never leave chopsticks upright in rice.
❤️
Health & Safety ▾
  • Water: Bottled water only. Avoid ice at street stalls.
  • Food: Street food is safe where it's busy and freshly cooked. Follow the locals.
  • Vaccinations: Consult your doctor — Hepatitis A, Typhoid, and Tetanus are common. Not medical advice.
  • Insurance: Strongly recommended with emergency medical evacuation cover. Confirm it covers trekking.
  • Mosquitoes: Use DEET repellent at dawn and dusk near rivers and rice paddies.
